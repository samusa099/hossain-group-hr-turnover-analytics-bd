# Assessment Rubric

## Scoring framework

| Evaluation area | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Data validation and calculation accuracy | 20% |
| Analytical segmentation and depth | 15% |
| Evidence versus assumption discipline | 15% |
| Decision usefulness and communication | 15% |
| Business interpretation | 15% |
| Recommendation quality | 10% |
| Data-gap and measurement plan | 5% |
| Ethical and responsible HR analytics | 5% |

## 1. Data validation and calculation accuracy — 20%

Strong submissions:

- identify and use the correct source files;
- validate required fields and date logic;
- reconcile reported and calculated indicators;
- distinguish counts from rates;
- explain material discrepancies;
- maintain reproducible logic.

## 2. Analytical segmentation and depth — 15%

Strong submissions:

- select relevant available segments;
- avoid relying only on company averages;
- compare absolute volume and proportional exposure;
- identify concentration without overstating causation;
- connect analysis to the selected role.

## 3. Evidence versus assumption discipline — 15%

Strong submissions clearly separate:

- supported findings;
- plausible but unverified explanations;
- matters that cannot be assessed.

The phrase **Insufficient evidence to conclude** should be used where necessary.

## 4. Decision usefulness and communication — 15%

Strong submissions:

- present a clear executive position;
- prioritise rather than list every possible issue;
- communicate uncertainty without avoiding a decision;
- use dashboards, tables or visuals only where they improve the decision;
- reflect the authority of the selected role.

## 5. Business interpretation — 15%

Strong submissions:

- explain the organisational meaning of workforce movement;
- consider recruitment, retention, structure, policy and governance together;
- recognise structural and potentially preventable movement;
- avoid treating one metric as a complete explanation.

## 6. Recommendation quality — 10%

Recommendations should define:

- action;
- owner;
- timing;
- evidence required;
- success measure;
- risk of inaction.

## 7. Data-gap and measurement plan — 5%

Strong submissions identify the minimum additional evidence needed and propose a practical 90-day and 180-day review framework.

## 8. Ethical and responsible HR analytics — 5%

Strong submissions:

- protect employee privacy;
- avoid individual judgement;
- do not present correlation as causation;
- do not fabricate missing data;
- verify current legal claims through official sources;
- identify the dataset as synthetic.

## Major deductions

| Issue | Deduction |
|---|---:|
| Claiming bias, discrimination or misconduct without supporting evidence | −15% |
| Treating exit count as turnover rate | −10% |
| Presenting correlation as causation | −10% |
| Inventing salary, attendance, performance, cost or legal evidence | −10% |
| Failing to disclose material limitations | −10% |
| Using unverified legal authority as a final source | −10% |
| Modifying files outside the permitted submission path | Submission rejected |
| Including credentials, private keys or real employee data | Submission rejected |
| Presenting synthetic data as authentic company information | Submission rejected |

## Review outcome

| Outcome | General interpretation |
|---|---|
| Accepted | Defensible, evidence-led and management-ready |
| Accepted with observations | Substantively sound with limited improvement areas |
| Revision required | Material analytical, evidence or communication issues remain |
| Not eligible | Submission violates scope, security, ethics or file policy |
