# Case Narrative

## The Monday Review

At 8:10 a.m. on a Monday in July, the Managing Director of Hossain Group received three documents before the weekly executive meeting.

The first was an operations note stating that several teams continued to report manpower pressure even after repeated recruitment approvals. The second was a finance summary questioning why replacement hiring remained high while the official headcount had increased. The third was an HR email containing a mixture of resignation comments, informal complaints and requests for additional workforce data.

None of the documents described the same problem in the same way.

Operations described missed handovers, attendance concerns and uneven employee commitment. Finance described recruitment activity that did not appear to produce equivalent workforce stability. Department heads referred to market competition, role scarcity and employee expectations. HR noted that some employees had raised concerns about career movement, supervisor conduct, workplace flexibility and inconsistent application of routine decisions.

The available comments were not collected through one controlled process. Some were formal. Some were exit reasons. Some were management opinions. Some were repeated statements with no supporting record.

During the meeting, the Managing Director asked a simple question:

> “Are we looking at normal workforce movement, or are we repeatedly replacing a problem that we have not identified?”

No one answered directly.

The Chief Operating Officer argued that the business required tighter discipline and stronger line supervision. The Finance Head asked whether recruitment was supporting growth or merely replacing exits. Several department heads said that competitor demand and external opportunities were beyond management control. HR cautioned that the current records could show patterns but could not automatically prove why those patterns existed.

The discussion then widened.

Management wanted to know whether the issue involved recruitment quality, retention, workforce structure, contract management, policy consistency, leadership practice, employee experience or weak HR information governance. Questions were also raised about attendance and leave control, promotion transparency, grievance handling and the organisation’s alignment with current Bangladesh labour-law and regulatory requirements.

The Managing Director did not approve another general recruitment request.

Instead, the executive team issued a formal review mandate. The existing Hossain Group project files would be treated as the evidence base. No additional internal records would be assumed. Any legal or regulatory statement would require independent research from current official sources. Any conclusion unsupported by available evidence would have to be identified as uncertain.

Before leaving the room, the Managing Director added one final instruction:

> “Do not bring me a dashboard that only describes the past. Bring me a position that management can defend, a decision we can act on, and a clear record of what we still do not know.”

That instruction defines the case.

## Your position

You are participating in the review as a selected decision-maker or specialist. You may work as a Managing Director, HR Business Partner, People Analytics professional, Operations and Finance representative, Policy and Governance reviewer, or a declared hybrid role.

Your task is not to confirm the loudest explanation. Your task is to determine which management concerns are supported, which remain plausible but unverified, which cannot be assessed from the current evidence and which actions are justified now.

## Evidence rule

Use only the existing project data and analytical files for quantitative findings.

Do not invent salary, attendance, performance, promotion, grievance, manager, productivity, labour-market, recruitment-cost or financial data.

Where a required conclusion cannot be supported, state:

> **Insufficient evidence to conclude.**
